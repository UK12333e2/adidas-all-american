adidas All American

Preservation of U.S. profits under a 100% import duty from 1 January 2027

Every pair pays double at the border. adidas can still win the U.S.

A 100% duty on all footwear and apparel imports would wipe out adidas's U.S. profit three times over. We use the tariff as an opportunity to build a stronger American customer base while protecting margins: defend margins by shrinking the dutiable base, recover margins by restoring profit per pair, and build DTC through adidas All American, our member app. Everything below runs on our operating model.

    So what

    The implication for the case

    A 100% duty lands on a market that is flat in units, almost wholly imported, already discounting its lifestyle stock, and sensitive to price. No brand is exempt, so the shock is symmetric; the duty is regressive, biting hardest on cheap shoes. Protect entry price points and let category elasticity, not brand switching, drive the volume case. Growth will come from mix and performance, not volume.

    A 100% tariff is a 20% shelf-price problem

    Duty is charged on customs value, never on retail price. On a premium shoe it adds about 20% of shelf price; on a $40 kids' shoe about 35%. That is why the entry tier is protected at 60–70% pass-through.

    The point: on the $140 shoe the duty takes about 92% of adidas's wholesale gross profit but only 35% of its DTC gross profit. Wholesale cannot survive without repricing; DTC can carry part of the cost. And because the duty is charged on factory price, cheap shoes are hit hardest: entry and children's ranges lose the most volume.

    Impact on adidas: absorbing the duty is never viable

    Three things that hold in every case

    1. Absorbing the duty is never viable: U.S. profit is negative in every case.
    2. Moving production abroad never helps, whatever the elasticity: every origin is taxed.
    3. Cutting customs value pays back in every scenario: its benefit scales with the duty, not with shoppers' behaviour.

    Cash

    Duty is paid at entry and sits in inventory for about 165 days. Before any response that ties up about €0.8bn of extra working capital in 2027 (€1.8bn × 165/365) Estimate. Under the recommended plan, with a smaller customs value and fewer units, the model's 2027 inventory is €1,759m against €1,287m without the duty: about €0.5bn. Foreign-trade zones defer it, from 30% of volume in 2028 to 60% from 2029.

    Revenue, margin and profit by scenario

    U.S., €m. Gross margin falls even in a good outcome: passing duty through in dollars protects profit but dilutes the ratio, so we judge success in operating profit and cash.

    Revenue holds, margin does not: the retailer margin mode and elasticity decide whether 2027 profit stays positive. Absorbing means a €1.3bn U.S. loss in 2027; price alone recovers most of it but costs about a sixth of volume.

    Our strategy: pay less duty, recover the rest, carry it direct

    Three strategies, each answering one question. Strategy 1 makes strategy 2's price rises smaller; strategy 2 funds and buys time for strategy 3; strategy 3 carries the duty through the channel that can bear it.

    The problem, and which strategy answers it

    From 1 January 2027: an extra 100% on the factory value of every imported shoe and garment, from every country, for at least five years. Absorbing it means a U.S. loss of about €1.4bn a year.

    ● main target · ○ also helps

    How they fit together: strategy 1 lowers the value the duty is charged on. Strategy 2 decides who pays what remains. Strategy 3 earns more from each customer in the channel that can carry the duty, and is the only one that keeps compounding after the five years. Each uses a capability adidas already has, applied at a new scale with a tariff-specific target.

    Method, risks and sources

    Value is the net present value of the difference in U.S. free cash flow between our plan and absorbing the duty, 2027–2031, at 8.5%. No group DCF and no market multiples: they value adidas's equity, not the tariff's effect.

    How the engine works

    customs value = in-scope revenue × cost-of-sales ratio × factory share = €1,827m for 2026E.

    net price change (tier) = pass-through × duty intensity × (1 − value cut)(1 − supplier share)(1 − U.S.-made share); the entry tier is protected at 60% pass-through.

    shelf change = net change × channel factor (retailer keeps dollars: 50% of list; DTC sells 15% below list).

    units = market × (1 + elasticity × shelf change), elasticity −0.7.

    opex = 60% fixed (inflating 2%) + 40% variable; DTC shift adds 5% margin on shifted revenue.

    inventory = (cost + duty × (1 − FTZ share)) × days ÷ 365; FCF = profit after tax + D&A − capex − Δ inventory.

    Key assumptions

    What a judge might ask

    Why not move production to Mexico or India? The case taxes every origin; rerouting doesn't change origin, and misdeclaring it is fraud.

    Why not reshore at scale? Under 1% of U.S. footwear is made domestically; adidas's Atlanta Speedfactory closed in 2020. U.S. production is a stage-gated option, not the plan.

    Is first-sale valuation safe? A bill to end it is pending, so we cap the lever and seek binding rulings.

    What's the biggest swing factor? Whether retailers keep dollar or percentage margins: +20% or +40% on the shelf.
